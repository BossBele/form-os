A form is not markup.It is a configuration.

Once a form is a file rather than a pile of components, everything else follows. You can version it, diff it, generate it, check it before it ever reaches a browser, and hand it to a colleague who does not write code. These four pieces are the whole system.

  1. 01 · the form itself

    FormConfig

    A plain file listing the fields, the rules, and the conditions. Readable by anyone, reviewable in a pull request, storable anywhere.

  2. 02 · the contract

    @form-os/schema

    Checks that a configuration is well formed before anything tries to render it — which is what lets a form be written by a tool, an API, or a person.

  3. 03 · the rules

    @form-os/core

    Answers one question, quickly and identically everywhere: what should this field be doing right now, given what has been filled in so far?

  4. 04 · the picture

    @form-os/react

    Takes those answers and draws real inputs on the screen, wired into react-hook-form and yours to style however you like.

portable by design

One configuration. Every platform.

The engine is plain JavaScript — there is no framework inside it. Describe the form once; only the renderer changes when you move between web, mobile, and whatever ships next.

an honest comparison

What this is, and what it is not.

form-os is not the first attempt at this, and the alternatives are not bad software. Most of them are very good at a slightly different job. Here is the difference, stated fairly.

Where the form actually lives

form-osIn a file. A description you can commit, review, diff, and hand to someone who does not write code.

react-hook-formIn the components you wrote by hand — which means the form only exists in one place, and only as long as that code survives.

Conditional behaviour

form-osDeclared on the field. Show, hide, require, disable and lock are five lines of data, and rules of the same kind combine automatically.

react-hook-formWritten as watchers and ternaries inside the component. Perfectly workable, and rewritten from scratch every time a form is built.

What it is, underneath

form-osA superset. The React binding is built on top of react-hook-form, so every hook it gives you is still there, with the per-form wiring removed.

react-hook-formThe foundation itself. Excellent at what it does — form-os would not exist without it — but it deliberately leaves the shape of your form to you.

How much you own

form-osEverything. Components come from your design system, registered once in a provider, and the defaults are yours to replace or ignore.

JSON FormsThe renderer decides what your inputs look like, and changing that means working against it rather than with it.

What you send to the browser

form-osA dependency-free core that runs in a test, a worker, or a server, plus the fields you actually use.

JSON FormsA renderer suite with its own component library, most of which a given form will never touch.

Who holds the keys

form-osYou do. Forms are files in your repository, and responses land wherever you point them.

Hosted form buildersThe vendor does. Forms live in their editor, at their URLs, priced per response — and leaving means rebuilding every one of them.

react-hook-form

A dependency, not a competitor. form-os is built on top of it.

JSON Forms

Closest in spirit, and heavier in practice. The idea is right; the renderer gets in the way.

Hosted form builders

The fastest way to start and the hardest way to leave. Worth it for some teams, and worth knowing the trade.

pricing

Free where it should be. Paid where it saves you work.

The library is MIT licensed and always will be — everything you need to build is in the free package. The paid tiers exist for the work around the library: hosting, analytics, compliance, and the migration nobody wants to do by hand.
